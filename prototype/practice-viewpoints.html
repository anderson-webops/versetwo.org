05 / Storytelling & collaboration · about 5 minutes

Same moment. Two viewpoints.

Invented rehearsal scene

Alex thinks rehearsal begins at four. Jordan thinks it begins at five. They are reading different versions of a message. Neither has checked which version is current.

  1. Write, speak privately or imagine two lines from Alex's view. What does Alex know? What is still an assumption?
  2. Try two lines from Jordan's view. Let the difference come from information, not a stereotype about either person.
  3. Choose what a listener should understand that the characters do not yet know.
  4. Write one useful question the collaborators could ask to check the plan.
Compare one possible pair

Alex: “The saved note says four; I’m ready by the door.”
Jordan: “My screen says five; I think I’m right on time.”

These original fictional lines show why each person might believe the schedule. A useful question is: “Which message is the latest, and can we confirm the time together?”
