College, practice and the evidence limit

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics says musicians and singers typically do not need postsecondary education, while emphasizing training and regular practice. This broad occupation description does not tell a minor to leave secondary school.

A 2024 review of hip-hop education identifies both possibilities and implementation problems, including adults imposing their own version of hip-hop. It does not evaluate this website. We are testing whether the resource is respectful, clear and useful; no lasting school outcome is claimed.

A connection you can choose

These activities give you room to decide what, if anything, helps your music. The learning routes are suggestions. You can practice on your own, ask a willing collaborator, use a library or community program, or choose a class or tutor.

Research on relevance in laboratory tasks and optional online training comes from different settings and has mixed results. It has not tested this website or shown that it improves school outcomes. If an activity adds pressure or makes your goal feel harder, you can shorten it, choose another task or stop.

Questions from classroom research

In Jabari Evans’s study of a school music program, students discussed mentorship and having their interests taken seriously. The author had no individual achievement measures outside the program.

Lauren Leigh Kelly’s 2020 classroom-study abstract describes a semester exploring identity and curriculum co-created with young people. We could read the abstract; the full methods remain unavailable.

These studies did not test this website. You should be able to choose a different artist, skip a story, question an activity or say a connection feels forced. A useful question for us is: “What would you change so this helps with your own goal?”

Why this project exists

Jacob's starting point was his sister's conversations with some young people while she worked in a juvenile defenders' office. Some said a future in rap made school unnecessary. Those observations do not show how common that belief is.

This resource is for people interested in the work behind rap and learning options that fit their goals. It does not assume someone's beliefs from race, income or justice status. The initial age assumption is 13–18 in the United States.

The name is still open. Artists and organizations have not endorsed the project. No partnership is implied. This is a local review prototype.
