# Original activities and optional seven-day plan

Implemented as local review content, not a tested curriculum. No grades, performances or uploads required. Examples and scenarios below are original. Work can be private, fictional, handwritten, spoken, read aloud with support or silently considered. No language variety is treated as defective.

## Six short activities

1. **Four lines, two versions** (implemented in prototype). Draft an invented scene; keep a first version, with an optional pause/return; change one detail, pause or unnecessary word; explain what changed. Compare with a willing listener if useful. Education connection: ask a teacher/tutor to compare clarity in two versions of a paragraph. Revision is a choice, not automatic improvement.
2. **The $500 show** (implemented). Invented fee $500, travel $75, engineer $125; $300 cash remains; retain $100 agreed project reserve; distribute $200 equally, $100 each. Reserve is retained cash, not automatically an expense. This does not calculate taxable profit or final income. Education connection: explain arithmetic and ask who approves expenses/terms. No real contract advice.
3. **Move the emphasis** (implemented). Original sentence: “We can start the track tomorrow.” Underline WE, START or TOMORROW in turn. Speak privately or write what each emphasis implies. Does a pause change it? Try one own line. Education connection: compare interpretations of a speech or drama/reading assignment with a teacher. Different speech/access needs welcome; no audio needed. Timing and emphasis are only part of flow.
4. **A reference that earns its place** (implemented with NASA/ESA offline cards). Choose a fact about a topic you like. Find an original source and a second credible source; check whether the second independently confirms it or merely repeats the first. Note source titles, dates and what each supports. Put the verified idea into two original lines, then explain why it belongs. Offline: a facilitator can provide sourced extracts or use library books. Education connection: ask a librarian/subject teacher about reliability. Clearly imagined scenes do not need to pretend to be history.
5. **Same moment, two viewpoints** (implemented). Fictional scene: two friends disagree about a rehearsal time. Write two lines from each perspective, without deciding who is the villain. What does each person know or assume? Education connection: compare point of view in a reading or narrative assignment. No personal conflict disclosure; narrative is one form of excellence, not a required style of rap.

6. **Choose a part, move it around** (implemented). Make four original phrases/patterns; choose one to repeat; compare two placements or pauses; explain the difference. Worked examples are ours. Solo, written/silent use and optional willing feedback work without audio tools. Learning connection: discuss repetition, arrangement or a pause with a teacher/mentor. Linked to Badu's retrospective production-lesson account without claiming her method or endorsement.

All activities now invite the learner's own connection before offering a route to adapt or skip. “No connection” is acceptable; practice has artistic value without a school conclusion. The writing activity links to the checked Little Simz return-to-draft story; neither activity nor waiting interval is prescribed by the artist.

## Optional seven-day plan

Implemented at prototype/week.html as a flexible review proposal, not a challenge requiring seven perfect days, an enrollment instruction or an evaluated intervention. Move days, shorten tasks or stop. Choose a manageable music goal and one realistic learning/support goal; no accountability data is collected.

| Day | Music practice | Learning or support action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose one skill you want more control over | Choose one learning question; identify a reliable person who could help |
| 2 | Collect three ordinary details | Read or listen to a short passage; note one question for a teacher/librarian |
| 3 | Try two lines with a factual reference | Check what each source supports; ask for source feedback |
| 4 | Make a second version of four lines | Ask about clarity in a real paragraph, presentation or assignment |
| 5 | Explain the invented show-budget split | Practice the related arithmetic or ask a tutor for help |
| 6 | Finish, speak privately or silently review a short piece, or compare two arrangements | If useful, try independent practice, a library resource, collaborator/tutor help, an assignment or a counselor question |
| 7 | Compare what changed and what you kept | Review what helped and what blocked progress; choose a realistic next step or none |

If not enrolled, use an appropriate education-worker contact rather than assuming an equivalency program fits. Transport, safety, learning access and immediate work needs require support, not only motivation. Ask a qualified person about locally appropriate options. No promise of artistic or educational outcomes.
