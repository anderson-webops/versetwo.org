# Study methods audit - first local increment

Checked October 2, 2026. These notes support adult editorial/pilot review. Directly inspected original publications replace imported citation markers for the narrow findings below. They do not validate this website. Inspection locations are printed PDF pages where stated; extraction lines are navigation aids only. No participant information was collected.

## Classroom evidence

**Adjapong and Emdin (2015), Rethinking Pedagogy in Urban Spaces**, Journal of Urban Learning, Teaching, and Research 11, 66-77. [Original paper](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1071416.pdf). Inspected pp. 69-76.

| Field | Audit |
|---|---|
| Design/setting | One US grade-six science classroom; teacher-researcher, call-and-response and student co-teaching. |
| Sample | 31 questionnaire respondents; focus groups of 2-5 selected using questionnaire responses. School enrollment of 486 is not the study sample. Exact ages and intervention duration not established. |
| Measures | Likert/open responses, focus groups, video, observations and notes; thematic coding. |
| Findings | Authors describe participation, perceived understanding and agency through observations/student accounts. |
| Comparison/uncertainty | No comparison group, random assignment, independent achievement test or longitudinal school outcome reported. Teacher/researcher roles and selected examples constrain inference. |
| Transfer | Grade-six, teacher-supported classroom activity differs from this voluntary ages-13-18 website. No justice-involved sample established. |

**Our application:** Ask whether a learner can explain a chosen reference or change, and what support helped. Keep explanation separate from perceived enjoyment; neither demonstrates durable educational improvement. Do not adopt demographic generalizations or assume a learner's musical identity. Rounded attitude percentages are omitted; they do not measure the target belief's prevalence.

## Contrary evidence and implementation difficulty

**Kelly and Sawyer (2019), When Keeping It Real Goes Wrong**, IASPM Journal 9(2), 6-21; published December 24, 2019. [Journal record](https://iaspmjournal.net/index.php/IASPM_Journal/article/view/937), [original PDF](https://iaspmjournal.net/index.php/IASPM_Journal/article/download/937/pdf/5857). Inspected pp. 9-16.

| Field | Audit |
|---|---|
| Design | Two teachers' critical-reflexive classroom narratives; not a controlled outcome evaluation. |
| Settings/sample | Kelly: semester elective, grades 10-12, 10 initially enrolled, 8 finally enrolled plus one intermittent non-enrolled participant. Sawyer: school assigned 25 students to a reengagement program. Distinct contexts, not a combined analytic sample. |
| Evidence | Teacher accounts, dialogue and reflective journal examples; no pooled effect estimate or comparative attendance/graduation result established. |
| Complications | Musical familiarity did not ensure discussion engagement; some learners wanted more guidance. Sawyer describes privileging his preferred older lyricists over students' preferences. |
| Limits | Subjective teacher positionality, small contextual narratives and different enrollment arrangements limit transfer. Program assignment is not our voluntary pilot model. |

**Our application:** Offer a concrete starting example without forcing a preferred artistic style. Ask what felt irrelevant, imposed or confusing. A learner may prefer direct help, beat-focused practice or no activity; disagreement is useful feedback rather than a failed participant.

## Synthesis and its sampling boundary

**Laforgue-Bullido, Abril-Hervás and Malik-Liévano (2024), Hip-hop and education: A literature review of experiences**, published July 22, 2024. [Original article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11159-024-10069-7). Methods, classification and limitations inspected.

| Field | Audit |
|---|---|
| Scope | Publications from 2012-2022; title-keyword criteria, peer-reviewed journal restriction and English/Spanish/Portuguese/Italian language restriction. Five databases. |
| Flow/unit | 175 search results, 82 in-depth reads, 68 included articles; 49 case studies. Article counts are not participant counts or intervention successes. |
| Analysis | Descriptive/thematic classification; no pooled causal effect size or website evaluation. |
| Limits | Availability/title/language restrictions can miss relevant work. Authors identify unclear definitions, uneven documentation outside formal education and distance from the US context. |

**Our application:** Use this review to locate original studies and design questions, not to promise that hip-hop content improves school outcomes. This first audit is not an exhaustive update of the research corpus; the exact Evans and Kelly matches below extend this first audit.

## Pilot evidence decision

The pilot can assess comprehension, usability, perceived respect, chosen action and reasons for declining. Those are formative measures. An activity completion or selected next step is not proof of attendance, attainment, reengagement or justice outcomes. Any later outcome study needs an explicit design, appropriate comparison and qualified review. The host must handle consent, safeguarding and practical support before involving minors.
